Favorite Books

A personal collection of works that matter

Below is a curated collection of favorite books spanning philosophy, literature, history, and more. This list will grow and evolve over time.

The Brothers Karamazov

Fyodor Dostoyevsky
Fiction / Philosophy
Dostoyevsky's final novel features three brothers, each representing different approaches to faith, doubt, and morality. Considered one of the greatest novels ever written, it grapples with questions of suffering, freedom, and God.

Fathers and Sons

Ivan Turgenev
Fiction / Philosophy
A novel examining the generational conflict between liberal romantics of the 1840s and the radical nihilists of the 1860s. Turgenev's nuanced portrayal of Bazarov explores the clash between old and new worldviews.

Notes from Underground

Fyodor Dostoyevsky
Philosophy / Fiction
A short but profound work presenting a narrator's bitter reflections on rationalism, free will, and the human condition. Often considered the first existentialist novel, it challenges Enlightenment assumptions about human nature.

Thus Spoke Zarathustra

Friedrich Nietzsche
Philosophy / Parable
A philosophical novel presenting Nietzsche's ideas through the teachings of a wandering prophet. Features the concept of the Übermensch and explores themes of eternal recurrence, self-overcoming, and the creation of new values.

On the Genealogy of Morals

Friedrich Nietzsche
Philosophy
Nietzsche traces the origins of morality through history, arguing that our concepts of good and evil are products of power dynamics and resentment rather than universal truths. A genealogical investigation into Western values.

The Joyous Science

Friedrich Nietzsche
Philosophy
A transitional work where Nietzsche develops his mature philosophy. Introduces the idea of eternal recurrence and emphasizes life affirmation, intellectual freedom, and the creative potential of human existence.

Beyond Good and Evil

Friedrich Nietzsche
Philosophy
Nietzsche's critique of traditional morality and Western philosophy. He challenges the concept of good and evil as social constructs, advocating for a transvaluation of all values and the cultivation of the noble and strong.

Catch-22

Joseph Heller
Fiction / Satire
A darkly comic novel about American bombardiers in World War II. Heller captures the absurdity and bureaucratic madness of war, creating a masterpiece of satire that influenced generations of writers.

The Catcher in the Rye

J.D. Salinger
Fiction
A coming-of-age novel following Holden Caulfield as he navigates adolescence, alienation, and his search for authenticity in a world of "phonies." A landmark work in American literature capturing teenage angst and disillusionment.

The Sorrows of Young Werther

Johann Wolfgang von Goethe
Fiction / Philosophy
An epistolary novel presenting a young man's passionate, ultimately tragic love affair. Goethe's exploration of romantic idealism and emotional suffering became a defining work of the Sturm und Drang movement.

The Gnostic Gospels

Elaine Pagels
History / Religion
Pagels examines the recently discovered Nag Hammadi texts, exploring alternative early Christian movements and the development of orthodox Christianity. A groundbreaking work in understanding early religious history.

Adam, Eve, and the Serpent

Elaine Pagels
History / Religion
Pagels explores how early Christian interpretations of the Adam and Eve story shaped attitudes toward sexuality, power, and freedom. A scholarly examination of how religious texts were used to construct political and sexual hierarchies.

Crime and Punishment

Fyodor Dostoyevsky
Fiction / Philosophy
A psychological novel exploring guilt, morality, and redemption through the story of Raskolnikov, a troubled student who commits murder. Dostoyevsky's masterwork delves into the darkest corners of the human psyche.

Godel, Escher, Bach

Douglas Hofstadter
Science / Philosophy
A work of philosophy and science that explores the nature of consciousness and intelligence through the interplay of mathematics, art, and music. Hofstadter's masterpiece examines self-reference and recursion in a way that challenges conventional thinking.

The Lessons of History

Will and Ariel Durant
History
A distillation of the Durants' lifetime study of civilization. Drawing from their massive 11-volume "Story of Civilization," they extract timeless patterns about human nature, government, economics, and the cycles of history.

The Odyssey

Homer
Fiction / Epic
Homer's epic poem following Odysseus's ten-year journey home after the Trojan War. A foundational work of Western literature exploring themes of perseverance, cunning, loyalty, and the struggle between fate and free will.

The Lucifer Principle

Howard Bloom
Science / Philosophy
Bloom argues that the universe and human nature are fundamentally driven by competition, hierarchy, and self-assertion. An ambitious work connecting physics, biology, and human history through evolutionary principles.

Human, All Too Human

Friedrich Nietzsche
Philosophy
Nietzsche's break from his earlier romanticism, adopting a more psychological and aphoristic approach. He examines human behavior, morality, and culture with skepticism toward metaphysics and idealism.

Empire of the Summer Moon

S.C. Gwynne
History
The rise and fall of the Comanche empire and the tragic story of Cynthia Ann Parker. A gripping account of the clash between Native American and Euro-American civilizations on the American frontier.

The Republic

Plato
Philosophy
Plato's Socratic dialogue exploring justice, the ideal state, and the nature of reality. Introduces the allegory of the cave and the theory of Forms. A cornerstone of Western political philosophy.

One Flew Over the Cuckoo's Nest

Ken Kesey
Fiction
A novel about rebellion against institutional control in a psychiatric hospital. McMurphy's battle against Nurse Ratched became a cultural touchstone for questioning authority and the definition of sanity.

The Electric Kool-Aid Acid Test

Tom Wolfe
Non-fiction / Journalism
Wolfe's immersive account of Ken Kesey and the Merry Pranksters' cross-country bus trip experimenting with LSD. A defining work of New Journalism capturing 1960s counterculture.

The Structure of Scientific Revolutions

Thomas Kuhn
Science / Philosophy
Kuhn introduces the concept of paradigm shifts, arguing that scientific progress happens through revolutionary breaks rather than steady accumulation. Changed how we understand scientific change.

First Love

Ivan Turgenev
Fiction
A novella about a teenage boy's infatuation with a captivating young woman. Turgenev captures the intensity and confusion of first love with psychological precision and emotional honesty.

Hamlet

William Shakespeare
Fiction / Drama
Shakespeare's tragedy of a Danish prince paralyzed by doubt and introspection. One of literature's most complex characters grapples with revenge, mortality, and the corruption of power.

Desert Solitaire

Edward Abbey
Nature / Memoir
Abbey's account of his seasons as a park ranger in the Utah desert. A meditation on wilderness, solitude, and the threat of industrial civilization to the natural world.

The Selfish Gene

Richard Dawkins
Science / Biology
Dawkins argues that evolution operates at the level of genes, not individuals or species. Introduced the concept of memes and revolutionized how we understand natural selection.

Amusing Ourselves to Death

Neil Postman
Media / Philosophy
Postman argues that television's emphasis on entertainment has eroded public discourse and rational thought. A prescient warning about how media shapes consciousness.

1984

George Orwell
Fiction / Dystopian
Orwell's dystopian vision of totalitarian surveillance and thought control. A chilling exploration of how power manipulates truth, language, and memory to maintain control.

Animal Farm

George Orwell
Fiction / Satire
An allegorical novella satirizing the Russian Revolution and Stalinist totalitarianism. Animals overthrow their human farmer only to recreate the same oppressive hierarchy.

Brave New World

Aldous Huxley
Fiction / Dystopian
Huxley imagines a future where humanity is controlled through pleasure, genetic engineering, and conditioning rather than pain. A different dystopian vision than Orwell's, equally disturbing.

An Imaginary Tale: The Story of √-1

Paul Nahin
Mathematics / History
Nahin traces the history of the imaginary unit and complex numbers. A fascinating account of how mathematicians reluctantly accepted and eventually embraced a number that "shouldn't exist."

Civilization and Its Discontents

Sigmund Freud
Philosophy / Psychology
Freud examines the tension between individual desires and societal demands. He argues civilization requires suppressing primal instincts, creating inevitable psychological discontent.

Relativity: The Special and General Theory

Albert Einstein
Science / Physics
Einstein's own accessible explanation of his revolutionary theories. He walks readers through the concepts of space-time, gravity as curvature, and the nature of the universe.

Walden

Henry David Thoreau
Nature / Philosophy
Thoreau's account of two years living in a cabin at Walden Pond. A meditation on simple living, self-reliance, and resistance to materialism. The grandfather of American nature writing and a foundational text for questioning industrial society.
